All work Vitality Group International · 2017 — 2018

One rewards app, rebranded for insurers in 10+ markets.

I designed features for Vitality's white-label health and rewards app: a single native iOS and Android product that insurance partners from Japan to Canada re-skin as their own, to get members moving and keep them engaged.

Role
UX/UI Designer
Timeline

Platform

Industry

Tools

At a glance

Vitality runs the world's largest behavioural health platform. Instead of each insurer building its own app, Vitality ships one core product that partners brand and configure. My job was to make that shared product feel native in every market, and motivating enough that people keep coming back.

25%Increase in user retention
15%Fewer support enquiries
10+Partner markets on one codebase
Context

Built once, shipped under many brands.

Each partner has its own brand, language, rewards catalogue and regulations, but runs on the same app.

The problem

Two sets of needs, one product.

Business

Partner markets needed a native app they could brand to their own requirements, which would win new clients and keep engagement high across their products, programme and app.

  • One platform that can be distributed, reused and customised globally.
  • Better user acquisition and engagement.

Members

Members wanted guidance, support and motivation to stay healthy, plus trusted health education, all in one app that tracks their health data in one place.

  • Ongoing motivation to keep a fit, healthy lifestyle.
  • One place to learn and track all health and fitness data.
My role

From team member to sole designer and design advocate.

I joined a design team working with development, marketing and product. As the project went on and the team changed, I became one of the last designers left, and took on championing user-centred design across the product.

  • User research & testing
  • Wireframes
  • iOS & Android UI
  • Functional prototypes
  • Illustration
Constraints

Shifting teams, many time zones, many stakeholders.

Frequent team changes meant rebuilding working relationships with new developers across time zones. With so many partner markets involved, reaching alignment and making decisions across regions was a challenge of its own.

Process

A loop, not a line.

Five phases that fed back into each other. Test findings and live feedback regularly sent work back to design.

  1. 01Define & research
  2. 02Sketch & design
  3. 03Review, test & iterate
  4. 04Check & test
  5. 05Update & improve
01 · Define & research

Understand every market before designing for any of them.

Problem statement

Framed a problem statement that bridged business goals and member needs.

Partner markets

Researched what each partner needed in functionality and customisation, plus each country's culture and preferences.

Competitors & trends

Mapped competitors' strengths and weaknesses and current mobile UI/UX patterns.

User mapping workshops

Worked with development and product to map the full user journey and the problem being solved.

02 · Sketch & design

Many ideas fast, then one agreed direction.

We started on whiteboards and paper, using team exercises like Crazy 8s to sketch many ideas in a short time. Each person presented, the team dot-voted on the strongest concepts, and we agreed a direction together.

The app's visual style was already set when I joined, so I turned the sketches into high-fidelity screens using the existing styles and components, and added to that library as new patterns were needed.

03 · Review, test & iterate

Test with members, align with every department.

Regular review sessions made sure every department was confident in the solution before it went further. User testing informed and validated design decisions throughout.

  • A/B testing
  • Card sorting
  • Moderated interviews & tests
Participants
6, mixed gender
Age range
18 — 65+
Format
Task-based, think-aloud
Scored on
Useful, usable, desirable, findable, accessible, credible, valuable, and "feels like Vitality"
04 · Check & test

Design QA before and after release.

Check. I ran QA sessions with testers and developers, reviewing built screens against the designs and flows and logging every spacing, placement and style difference.

Test. Once features reached production and live environments, I tested them in the app again. This second pass caught what builds missed and kept quality consistent across partner versions.

05 · Update & improve

From iteration 01 to iteration 50.

Business and member feedback kept the work moving. Key screens went through dozens of rounds before they settled.

The solution

A core app that partners make their own.

Key features

Know your health. Get active. Get rewarded.

Know your health

Personal goals from real assessments

Short assessments on stress, mental wellbeing and social support feed personal health and fitness goals. Goals are set to be realistic, so they encourage members rather than put them off.

Get active

Every workout counts toward status

Members earn points for any activity, from running to yoga. Points build an annual status, and higher status unlocks better rewards this year and next.

  1. BlueStarting status
  2. Bronze12,000 pts
  3. Silver20,000 pts
  4. Gold24,000 pts
Get rewarded

A weekly game with real prizes

Each week, members can play a game to win Vitality currency or real rewards like a Starbucks coffee or a $10 gift card from local brands.

Outcome

More members stayed, fewer needed help.

25%Increase in user retention
15%Decrease in support enquiries
What I learned
  • Design for localisation from day one. Layouts had to hold up in many languages and scripts, and content had to respect each culture.
  • White-label needs rules, not just themes. Clear component rules made it possible for partners to rebrand without breaking the experience.
  • Advocate when no one else will. Being one of the last designers on a large, global team taught me to own design quality end to end.
